Prep Time: 30 minutes

Cook Time: 1 hour

Serves: 6-8

{% include difficulty.html difficulty_level=4 %}

*Recipe adapted from [Simply Recipe](https://www.simplyrecipes.com/recipes/moms_turkey_stuffing/)*

---

### Ingredients
* Neck and giblets from 1 turkey (or 2–3 cups chicken stock)
* 1 cup walnuts
* 1 loaf day-old French or Italian bread, cut into 3/4-inch cubes (about 10–12 cups)
* 2 cups chopped onion
* 2 cups chopped celery
* 6 tablespoons unsalted butter, divided
* 1 green apple, peeled, cored, and chopped
* 3/4 cup currants or raisins
* 5–10 green martini or pimento olives, pimento removed, chopped
* 1/4 cup chopped fresh parsley
* 1 teaspoon poultry seasoning or ground sage, to taste
* Salt and freshly ground pepper, to taste

### Instructions
1. Make the stock (skip if using chicken stock): Put the turkey neck, heart, and gizzard in a small saucepan. Cover with a few cups of water and add a little salt. Bring to a simmer and cook uncovered for about 1 hour. Strain. You should have 2–3 cups of stock. (Chicken stock or plain water also works.)
2. Prepare the walnuts: Cover the walnuts with 1 inch of water in a small saucepan and boil for 3–4 minutes to remove bitter tannins. Drain and let dry. Toast in a frying pan over medium-high heat, stirring, until lightly browned (or microwave on high for 1–2 minutes until fragrant).
3. Toast the bread: Spread the bread cubes on a baking pan and dry in a 200°F oven for 10 minutes (skip if already very dry). Melt 3 tablespoons butter in a large sauté pan over medium heat. Add the bread and stir to coat. Let toast, turning only once a side is lightly browned.
4. Sauté the vegetables: In a large Dutch oven, sauté the onion and celery in the remaining 3 tablespoons butter over medium-high heat until cooked through, 5–10 minutes.
5. Combine: Add the toasted bread, walnuts, apple, currants or raisins, olives, and parsley. Stir in 1 cup stock. Season with poultry seasoning or sage, salt, and pepper.
6. Cook: Cover and reduce heat to low. Cook about 1 hour, until the apples are tender. Check every 15–20 minutes, adding stock or water as needed to keep it moist and prevent sticking (you'll likely need 1–2 more cups). The finished stuffing should be soft, not crispy, as if it had cooked inside the turkey.
